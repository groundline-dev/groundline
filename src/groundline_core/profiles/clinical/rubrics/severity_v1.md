# Severity rubric v1: how much would it matter if the note left this fact out?

Grade the consequence of the omission for the patient and for the next clinician who reads the note. Grade the omission, not how dramatic the topic sounds. Grade each fact on its own, as if it were the only thing missing from an otherwise complete note.

## Grades

**critical**: leaving it out could change what a clinician does, delay or misdirect care, or create a safety risk. Typical cases:
- the diagnosis or working impression, or a hedge on it (a "possible" diagnosis recorded as certain is just as serious)
- any drug, dose, frequency, duration, allergy or interaction
- a condition attached to the plan ("only if it gets worse")
- safety-netting advice: when to return, call, or seek urgent care
- an examination or test that was done, and its key result
- a pertinent negative the management decision rests on (no fracture on X-ray, so no immobilisation beyond a splint)

**supporting**: the note is less complete, clear or defensible without it, but what happens next is unlikely to change. Typical cases:
- onset, timing, mechanism or character details that colour the picture without deciding the plan
- what makes a symptom better or worse
- background history or social context with no immediate action attached
- a secondary symptom that neither supports nor threatens the diagnosis

**peripheral**: no plausible clinical consequence. Typical cases:
- small talk, rapport, thanks, administrative or dictation housekeeping ("order the medications we discussed")
- general education phrasing
- a restatement of something another fact on the list already says

## Procedure

Ask in order and stop at the first "yes":
1. **Action**: would a competent clinician reading the note plausibly do something different if this fact were missing? Yes → critical.
2. **Safety**: even if nothing changes today, does its absence remove a safety net or leave a hidden risk? Yes → critical.
3. **Record quality**: does its absence materially weaken the note as a clinical record? Yes → supporting.
4. Otherwise → peripheral.

If you are still torn between two grades, choose the lower one. Do not aim for any particular mix of grades.
